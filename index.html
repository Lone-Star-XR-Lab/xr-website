About the Lab

A hands-on space for immersive learning

The XR Lab is a space for exploring virtual reality, augmented reality, mixed reality, and other immersive technologies. Students, faculty, and staff use it for classes, projects, training, demonstrations, games, and independent exploration.

No previous XR experience is required. Games are a great way to get comfortable with the headset and controllers, and from there many students branch out into educational, creative, and simulation experiences.

The lab is staffed by Tylor Wurz, AR/VR Tech III.

What is XR?

Extended Reality (XR) is an umbrella term for three kinds of immersive technology:

  • Virtual Reality (VR): step into a fully digital world.
  • Augmented Reality (AR): layer digital content on top of the real world.
  • Mixed Reality (MR): digital objects that interact with your real surroundings.

Before You Visit

What to know before you put on a headset

Getting started

First time? No experience needed. We'll help with headset fit, controls, boundaries, and basic navigation.

Sign in: Students and staff sign in at the lab with their LSC ID or LSC email. Guests are welcome too.

Glasses: The headsets are set up to fit glasses, so wear yours.

Time: When the lab is busy, headset sessions may be limited to 30 minutes so everyone gets a turn.

Health, safety, and comfort

Motion sickness: Some people feel dizzy or nauseated in VR. Start with short, low-motion experiences and stop right away if you feel unwell.

Seizure warning: VR can trigger seizures or blackouts in people with photosensitive epilepsy. If you have a history of seizures, talk to lab staff or your doctor before using a headset.

Age: Headset users should be 10 or older, following Meta's age guidelines for Quest headsets. Younger children may use the lab with supervision from a parent or guardian.

Cleaning: Please wipe down the headset and controllers after each use with the alcohol wipes or spray provided. Staff also cleans each headset again after use. Lenses are cleaned separately without alcohol, which can damage them, so leave the lenses to staff. Headsets use silicone face gaskets that can be wiped clean, and disposable face masks are available if you would like one.

Comfort and accommodations: Many experiences can be used seated, and you can take a break or stop at any time. Alternative participation (such as watching and directing from an iPad) and extra breaks can be arranged. Students with an approved accommodation can also contact LSC Accessibility Services.

XR in the Classroom

How classes use the lab

You do not need to be an XR expert to bring a class in. The lab can help you find and test experiences, prepare equipment, introduce students to the headsets, cast headset views, and troubleshoot during the session.

Environmental Science

Wander

Students visit places around the world and compare how they have changed over the years, exploring the idea of shifting baselines.

Environmental Science

On the Ice: Hatched

Students step into the role of a scientist in Antarctica studying the lives of penguins.

Anatomy & Physiology

Awake Heart

Students explore the human heart up close and learn how its structures work.

English

Ho Chi Minh City in 360°

While reading The Best We Could Do by Thi Bui, students experience the sights and sounds of Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam.

History

1893 Chicago World's Fair

While reading The Devil in the White City by Erik Larson, students step into the World's Fair to experience what it was like to be there.

Class visits can range from a short demonstration to a full session. The lab runs 8 headsets at a time; larger classes rotate in groups, with other students watching on the iPads or Vibe Board or working on a related activity.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can I wear glasses with the headsets?

Yes. The lab's headsets are set up to fit glasses. Let us know if you need help getting a comfortable fit.

Do I need an appointment?

Not for students during Open Lab hours, as long as the lab isn't reserved. Classes, groups, and Friday visits need a booking.

Is it free?

Yes. The XR Lab is free to use.

Can I bring friends who aren't LSC students?

Yes. Guests are welcome in the lab.

How do I sign in?

Students and staff sign in at the lab with their LSC ID or LSC email.

How far ahead should I book a class visit?

At least 1 week. New apps that need testing or purchasing take longer, so schedule a consultation early in the semester if you can.

How many students can use the lab at once?

Eight people can be in headsets at the same time. Larger groups rotate, and others can watch the headset views on the iPads or Vibe Board.

What if I start to feel sick in VR?

Take the headset off, sit down, and let staff know. Discomfort usually passes in a few minutes. Slower, seated experiences are a good place to start.

Can I work on my own XR project in the lab?

Yes. Students are welcome to use the lab for class or personal XR projects during Open Lab hours. Please bring your own laptop for development work.

Can faculty or staff borrow a headset?

Meta Quest 2 headsets are available for faculty and staff checkout and testing. Email the lab to request one, and we'll work out the checkout length with you based on your project.
